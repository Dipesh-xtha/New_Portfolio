Bugv B2B Website Redesign.

Making Bugv’s security offerings easier for organizations to understand and explore, while keeping a distinct path for researchers.

01 / Project at a glance

A clearer route through a two-sided platform.

Bugv connects organizations seeking security testing with researchers who find and report vulnerabilities. This case study focuses on the B2B website experience.

ContextWebsite redesign for a cybersecurity platform with organization and researcher audiences.
Primary audienceOrganizations evaluating Bug Bounty, PTaaS, and VDP.
Secondary audienceSecurity researchers who need a separate, discoverable route.

02 / The problem

Two audiences, one unclear journey.

The research document’s review of the earlier site described messaging and navigation friction. These are heuristic observations, not findings from user interviews or usability tests.

A prospective buyer needed to answer three questions quickly.

  1. What does Bugv offer my organization?
  2. Which security model fits my need?
  3. Where do I go to discuss it?

General feature-led messaging, blended audience navigation, and scattered technical explanations made that sequence less direct in the review. The redesign organizes the buyer’s evaluation path and keeps a researcher route visible.

03 / Research & insights

From review to design direction.

Inputs: the supplied research document, its heuristic reading of the earlier site, and its competitive information architecture review. Its proposals and unverified claims are not treated as measured user behavior.

Decision 01

Separate the audiences

The earlier site mixed organization and researcher content in the same navigation context. Give organizations a product route and researchers their own entry point.

Decision 02

Name each offering

General security language made engagement models harder to distinguish. Introduce Bug Bounty, PTaaS, and VDP as distinct choices with dedicated pages.

Decision 03

Reveal detail at the right moment

Technical explanations were dispersed. Move from a simple overview to deeper product-specific details.

Decision 04

Connect proof to action

The competitive review highlighted trust cues and clear next steps. Place verifiable proof and contact actions near relevant explanations.

04 / Information architecture

Route by intent, then deepen the detail.

The new structure gives each audience a distinct path. A verified before-and-after sitemap was not available in the supplied material.

For organizations

Understand → compare → contact

  1. Homepage overview
  2. Bug Bounty, PTaaS, or VDP
  3. Product detail and pricing
  4. Request pricing or contact Bugv

For researchers

Discover the researcher experience

  1. Researcher entry point
  2. Researcher page
  3. Explore relevant programs
05 / Design decisions

What changed, and why.

Decision 01

Start with the buyer’s question

The homepage moves from what Bugv does to the problem, product choices, and next step. A first-time buyer can orient before going deeper.

Decision 02

Make the products comparable

A shared discovery area introduces Bug Bounty, PTaaS, and VDP; dedicated pages explain how each works.

Decision 03

Build trust without overclaiming

Process and credibility content support evaluation. Any public proof or product claim needs verification before publication.

Decision 04

Make the next action clear

Contact actions sit near relevant explanations. Pricing uses “Request pricing” rather than unverified prices.

Decision 05

Keep a visible researcher path

Researchers have a route that does not interrupt the organization’s product evaluation journey.
