Product design case study · Bugv · 02 / 03

Making identity verification clear, recoverable and trustworthy.

I redesigned Bugv’s single-page KYC form into a guided four-step journey with visible progress, clearer document requirements, review states and targeted correction.

01 / At a glance

A focused redesign of a high-trust product moment.

KYC is not only a data-entry task. Users need to know why information is required, what evidence is acceptable, what is happening after submission and how to recover when verification fails.

ContextAn identity-verification journey inside Bugv’s researcher experience.
RoleUI/UX · Product Designer
ScopeInitial KYC, progress, submission, review status, correction and resubmission.
Design workFlow audit, information architecture, interaction design, UI states and prototype.

Project duration and launch status need confirmation before publishing a timeline or business impact.

02 / The problem

The old experience looked like a profile form, not a verification journey.

Everything happened on one long page: identity details, contact information, uploads, processing messages and submission actions. The interface did not clearly communicate sequence, status or recovery.

Decision 01

No visible sequence

A long page made the task feel heavier and gave users no sense of progress.

Decision 02

Generic document request

“Upload document file” did not explain which evidence was accepted or how to prepare it.

Decision 03

Conflicting actions and status

Save, Submit and Under process competed without a clear primary next step.

Decision 04

No recovery route

The interface did not show what happens if only one document cannot be verified.

How might we turn KYC from a dense administrative form into a clear journey that prevents errors and helps users recover?

03 / Information architecture

A four-step journey, followed by meaningful status.

The structure separates data collection from system review. Bugv does not use biometric or liveness checks, so the flow focuses on information, documents, consent and transparent verification feedback.

01Personal information

Core legal and contact details.

02Identity verification

ID type and document evidence.

03Address verification

Residential information and evidence.

04Review & consent

Check, edit, consent, submit.

After submission

Under review → wait  ·  Verified → continue  ·  Action required → fix affected step → resubmit

04 / Design decisions

Each decision responds to a weakness in the old flow.

Decision 01

Progressive disclosure

Personal, Identity, Address and Review reduce what users must interpret at once, with one clear goal and primary action per step.

Decision 02

Document-specific guidance

Explicit requirements, front/back uploads where applicable, previews and validation feedback help users prepare usable evidence.

Decision 03

Review before commitment

A dedicated review stage groups information by step, offers direct Edit actions, and connects consent to submission.

Decision 04

Targeted correction

The experience names the exact issue, preserves accepted information, and returns the user to the affected step.

05 / System states

The interface explains what happened and what comes next.

The experience does not end at Submit. Dedicated states keep the system understandable while Bugv reviews the information.

Not started

Explain what is required before the user begins.

In progress

Preserve work and show the next unfinished step.

Under review

Confirm receipt and avoid duplicate submission.

Verified

Confirm completion and unlock the next action.

Action required

Name the issue and route to the affected section.

Re-verification

Confirm corrected evidence has returned to review.

07 / Outcome & reflection

A complete design system for verification.

The design deliverable covers initial verification, progress, document submission, review, verification status, correction requests and resubmission. Launch, handoff and measured business impact should only be added when confirmed.
